Scientists Publish New Study on Coffee and Heart Health

Researchers at a university medical centre have published findings from a five-year longitudinal study 
examining the relationship between moderate coffee consumption and cardiovascular outcomes. 

The study, which followed 12,000 participants aged 40-70, found a modest association between 
consuming two to three cups of coffee per day and a slightly reduced risk of certain heart conditions. 
However, the researchers cautioned that the study is observational in nature and does not establish 
a direct causal relationship.

Lead researcher Dr. Maria Chen said the team controlled for factors including smoking, exercise habits, 
and diet. She noted that the findings are consistent with several other studies in the field but 
emphasised that individuals with specific health conditions should consult their physician before 
making changes to their diet.

The study was published in the Journal of Cardiovascular Research and has been reviewed by independent 
experts in the field. Further research with randomised controlled trials would be needed to confirm 
the association.
